## 1. The job of the page

Take a clinic owner, or the person who runs a clinic's marketing, from "I've
never thought about this" to "I want to see what AI says about my clinic", and
get them to type their website. The money moment comes three screens later
(Rp99.000), so the page must also make the price and the free step obvious
before the click, so nobody feels tricked at checkout.

## 2. Who it is for

| Reader | What they care about | What they must believe before clicking |
|---|---|---|
| **Clinic owner** (often a dentist or doctor who runs the business) | New patients, the clinic's name, the clinic across the road, not wasting money, staying within health advertising rules | Patients ask AI about clinics like mine; that question is close to a booking; I can't see it anywhere; the report is concrete; it's honest; it's cheap and safe to try |
| **Clinic marketing team** | New channels, showing the owner something concrete, not looking bad | I'll find something useful; I can bring it to the owner; I get tasks I can actually do; a low score won't be blamed on me |

Most of them do not yet know that patients ask AI which clinic to choose. So
the page **educates before it sells**: it opens with a scene and a tension, not
with the product.

## 3. The big idea

**Patients no longer search. They ask. And AI answers with names.**

The hook: *Klinik Anda disebut AI, atau klinik sebelah?* (Is AI naming your
clinic, or the one next door?)

The tension only Nuave can point out: Indonesian health-advertising rules
(Permenkes 1787/2010) do not let a clinic call itself the best or compare itself
with other clinics. Patients still ask AI "which clinic is good?", and AI still
answers, compares and names clinics, using whatever public information it
finds. The clinic can't write that answer. It can only make sure the
information AI reads is clear and correct. (Plain observation, not legal
advice; Yasir checks the wording.)

## 5. How the page persuades

**Harry Dry's landing-page method (Marketing Examples), applied:**
title that names the problem for cold readers → subtitle that says how →
the real product in use as the visual → believability → a call to action with
a reason → features and objections → proof → FAQ → a second call to action
that reminds why → founder's note. Every line was checked against his three
tests: can you picture it, can it be proven true or false, and could nobody
else say it.

**NLP language patterns, used honestly.** The copywriter's full map is in
`COPY.md` Part E. The main ones:

| Pattern | Example on the page |
|---|---|
| Agree first, then lead (pacing and leading) | "Google Maps sudah rapi. Ulasan terus masuk. Instagram jalan tiap minggu." → "Namanya: jawaban AI." |
| Presupposition (treat a true thing as given) | "Sekarang pasien tanya AI dulu, baru chat admin." |
| Concrete, sensory detail | "Pasang behel. Bisa cicil. Dokter perempuan. Sabtu sore, karena hari biasa kerja." |
| Chunking down (from "AI era" to one patient) | "Ada lokasi. Ada layanan. Ada cara bayar. Ada jadwal." |
| Reframe | "Orang yang bertanya sedetail ini bukan sedang iseng." · "Angka rendah bukan rapor merah." |
| Future pacing | "Bayangkan Anda bisa membaca sendiri jawaban AI..." |
| Reason why ("karena") | "Langkah pertama gratis, karena kami perlu menemukan klinik Anda sebelum bertanya ke AI." |
| Towards and away-from | "Klinik Anda disebut AI, atau klinik sebelah?" |
| Candour as trust | "Yang kami tunjukkan, dan yang tidak kami janjikan." |

Lines we will not cross: no fear-mongering, no countdowns or scarcity, no
invented numbers, no promise of patients, revenue or rankings.

## 6. Trust without customers

There are no customers, logos, ratings or testimonials yet, and none are
invented. Believability comes from:

1. **The real report format**, shown early (section 4) and in full (section 11), with a fictional clinic and a clear label.
2. **Method in plain sight**: 10 questions in Indonesian, asked without the clinic's name, to AI built by OpenAI with web search; every answer shown in full with its sources.
3. **A source under every number.**
4. **Saying what we don't do**, at the exact point of doubt (Google, rankings, medical quality, patient data).
5. **A real person**: the founder's note and a working support address.

## 7. One action, honest at every step

- One action name everywhere: **Cek klinik saya di AI** (proposal; the approved "Cek bisnis saya di AI" stays the control for a later test).
- The same sign-up box (website or Instagram + button) in the hero, after the blind spot, and at the end.
- Every box says the first step is free and only finds the clinic, and that the AI audit is Rp99.000, paid once. "Pratinjau gratis" (today's hero line) is retired because it suggests a free AI result.

## 9. Measuring and testing

Summary of [`GROWTH.md`](./GROWTH.md):

- **North star:** paid clinic reports opened per week. Landing metric: the share of visitors who start the form.
- **No analytics run on the site today**, so nothing on the page can be measured yet. Smallest fix: a tiny first-party event counter with no personal data, or Cloudflare Web Analytics.
- **Traffic is small (outreach-sized), so real A/B tests can only read big swings.** Before launch, de-risk with: a 5-second test and a preference test with 5 to 8 clinic owners, rotating outreach opening lines, and a "painted door" while orders are still gated.
- **Top tests later:** hero framing (tension vs a real patient question), an early report teaser, long vs short education by channel, the outreach hero variant, the sticky bar, and the CTA label.

5. Refund line (waits for S5).
