## 2. Language standard the founder applies

The most productive reading of the marks is the founder's own standard: *does
this resemble something a customer would actually type to get the answer they
want, with no editing needed?* The founder's edits confirm concrete signals:

1. **Ask about the property the buyer cares about.** "Kopi Sudut cocok nggak
   untuk orang yang cari kopi susu?" → "Kopi Sudut kopi susunya enak ngga?"
   Generic suitability wording hides a simpler desired answer (taste, price,
   speed, WiFi, service). Direct property questions are allowed when grounded.
   A taste question is *not* automatically a recommendation measurement —
   keep that distinction in coverage metadata.
2. **Remove context that does not help this request.** "recommended nggak buat
   ngopi enak" → "recommended nggak". Audience lists ("keluarga atau pekerja")
   and obvious use descriptions are surplus unless they change the answer.
3. **Use customer vocabulary, not taxonomy.** "kedai kopi susu" → "kafe" /
   "coffee shop". Evidence for this case, not a universal category mapping;
   changing the noun must not silently imply facilities (WiFi, seating).
4. **Generic shopping checklists miss the decision.** "Apa saja yang perlu
   dicek…" and "Bagaimana cara memilih laundry…" were replaced with a direct
   request. But advice questions are not banned outright — "Gimana cara tau
   laundry ini bagus atau engga?" was suggested as a direction — the
   distinction is a believable uncertainty vs an elaborate inspection
   checklist. That rewrite needs an explicit business identity for standalone
   use.
5. **Drawbacks should sound like a real concern.** "…untuk pekerja remote atau
   mahasiswa yang ingin bekerja siang hari" → "Apa review negatif dari Kopi
   Bukit Biru?"; "…kekurangan atau alasan untuk ragu…" → "Laundry Segar Kilat
   oke ngga?". Negative-review evidence and overall evaluation are different
   measurements — do not silently tag both as "drawback coverage"; distinguish
   asking whether negative reviews exist from asserting they do.
6. **Ordinary fragments are acceptable.** "Kafe yang recommended di Depok",
   "Rekomendasi laundry di Bekasi yang tepat waktu", "Berapa biaya Laundry
   Segar Kilat?" do not need to be complete formal sentences. Natural
   expression and intent matter, not token imitation of the founder's spellings
   or slang.

## 3. Validity constraints on the suggested rewrites

Recorded marks stay as recorded, but several rewrites would change context or
measurement; these are flagged in the calibration (label preserved, semantic
change noted separately):

- "Dekat kantor" still needs an office area; "Kopi Sudut cocok buat WFC ngga?"
  adds a scenario absent from the sparse Kopi Sudut brief.
- "Bandingin Kopi Sudut vs Starbucks di Depok" introduces a comparator and
  turns an unnamed question into a named one — cannot replace B Q5 under the
  6/4 allocation.
- "Manual brew Kopi Bukit Biru enak ngga?" changes a comparative work-fit
  question into a taste question.
- "Berapa biaya Laundry Segar Kilat?" removes the hidden-fee subquestion.
- "Laundry ini" requires a named referent.
- "Pickup/delivery ke rumah/kosan" must not introduce boarding-house geography
  as a new confirmed fact.
- Several Light edits remove criteria or broaden categories; the Light label
  is not proof of measurement equivalence.

## 4. What the earlier AI evaluation got wrong (and what still stands)

The AI was too willing to mark Keep because a question was grammatical,
answerable, and defensible as an audit question — confusing plausibility with
polished completeness. A question can be grounded and still feel artificial
because it includes information no customer would say. That said, the
multi-criteria request the founder kept ("WiFi cepat, colokan dekat meja,
suasana tenang, harga kopi masuk akal") is direct evidence **against** a rigid
word limit or a blanket ban on multiple criteria.

Earlier findings that still matter:

1. The current A pipeline replaced Q9 with deterministic repair in all three
   initial packs; the raw model comparisons were cleaner. Do not attribute the
   repaired wording to Luna.
2. Missing office context makes several A questions unexecutable (B handles
   that more honestly).
3. B still fills spare positions with generic advice, repeated discovery, and
   unnecessary audience descriptions.
4. All successful records use Luna; no Terra challenger exists — these results
   do not establish that another model would fix the problem.
5. The results include earlier A outputs and B schema failures — preserved as
   history; schema rejection is not a naturalness failure.
